# Indian ETFs and index funds

From *ETF* [5], *Index fund* [6], *Gold & silver stocks and ETFs* [49].

## ETF over index fund, on cost

The note's ordering is explicit: **index fund < ETF on charges**. Worked example —
Nippon India ETF Nifty 50 BeES beats UTI Nifty 50 Index Fund Direct Growth on charges.

Named index funds where a fund is preferred: Motilal Oswal Nifty Bank, Motilal Oswal Nifty 500.

## What to check before buying an ETF

1. **AUM** — size
2. **Expense ratio** — low
3. **Tracking error** — low
4. **Volume / liquidity** — verify someone is actually trading it at
   `nseindia.com/market-data/exchange-traded-funds-etf`
5. **iNAV** — buy at the indicative NAV shown on the NSE site, not blindly at market price

## Entry rule

**Buy when the index or sector falls 5% or more.** Named examples: Nifty 50 BeES, IT BeES
(*"Buy Itbees if it sector decrease below 5 or more than it will be more profit"*), Bank Nifty BeES.

## Gold and silver [49]

The note works through why **rate cuts push gold up**:

1. **Lower opportunity cost** — gold pays no interest, so falling deposit/bond returns make it
   relatively more attractive
2. **Weaker USD** — gold is priced in dollars, so a softer dollar makes it cheaper abroad
3. **Inflation hedge** — cuts stimulate borrowing and inflation
4. **Safe-haven demand** — cuts often signal slowdown fears

The page ends with two open questions the note-taker had not resolved: **the exceptions where
gold does not rise after a cut**, and **how to value gold**. Treat both as open.
