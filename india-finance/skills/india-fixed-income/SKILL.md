# Indian fixed income and cash

From *Fixed deposit FDs* [24], *Gilt or debt vs FD* [4], *Emergency fund* [79],
*Mutual funds money purse* [42], *Stocks for lending* [11].

## Deposit insurance

**₹5 lakh insured per individual per bank.** The notes reference Stable Money for comparing FD
rates across banks — spread across banks rather than concentrating above the limit.

## FD laddering

For money needed within one to two years, split rather than locking a single deposit:

```
FD 1 → 1 year 4 months
FD 2 → 2 years 2 days
FD 3 → 3 years
```

As each matures, **roll it into a fresh 3-year FD**, so after the ramp there is a maturity every
year at 3-year rates.

**Odd tenures are deliberate** — "1 year 2 days" or "1 year 10 days" rather than exactly a year,
because banks price specific buckets differently. Check the rate card for the exact break.

Before choosing a small finance bank (the note names Suryoday SFB), check the bank's own listed
stock performance as a solvency sniff test.

## Gilt and debt funds [4]

- Check **1-year performance and AUM**
- **Growth option only** — IDCW/interim payouts credit cash out and break compounding
- Taxed at salary slab, still generally better than FD after tax over longer holds
- Named: SBI Magnum Gilt Growth

## Idle cash

Money not needed for one to two months belongs in debt funds rather than a savings account —
safer than equity and better than savings, *after* accounting for tax and expense ratio.

## Emergency fund [79]

Allocated under a **40/30/20** split in the notes. The page is brief — the rule is captured, the
detail is not.
