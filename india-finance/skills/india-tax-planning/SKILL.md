# Indian tax planning for investments

From *Tax free 0% tax* [25], *Fixed deposit FDs* [24], *Mutual funds money purse* [42],
*Trading and intraday tax* [60], *ITR Filing* [27].

## Long-term capital gains

- **12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh** per year
- The exemption is annual — the notes' approach is to **realise gains each year** to use it,
  checking purchase dates in the broker's tax report
- After selling for the exemption you can re-buy, optionally in another broker app, if conviction
  holds. Buy first, then sell, if liquidity allows

## Harvesting and set-off

Worked example from the notes: on ₹2 lakh of gains, tax applies above ₹1.25L → ₹75k taxable
≈ **₹9k**, versus roughly **₹47k** without harvesting.

- Offset **LTCG profit against STCL loss**
- Losses carry forward **8 years**
- Set-off works **across stocks, mutual funds and ETFs** — they net against each other

## FIFO on partial sales — easy to get wrong

**Average price is not used.** If you bought 100 shares at one price and 200 at another, selling
150 takes the **first 100 at the original price** for tax purposes.

## Debt mutual funds

Taxed at your **income tax slab**, not under equity LTCG/STCG rules — because they are not
equity. Factor this in when comparing against FD returns.

## Fixed deposits

- Interest taxed at your slab
- Age 60+: no tax where interest is up to ₹50k
- The note leaves an **open question**: how FD interest stacks onto income below ₹12.5 lakh under
  the new regime, and the position for zero-income seniors. Verify before relying
