# Indian term and health insurance

From *Term Insurance & Other insurances* [20] and *Health insurance* [28].

## Term insurance

**Preference order in the notes:** Max Life → ICICI → HDFC.
HDFC is listed first by familiarity but the note says it has *"less features than others"*; Max
Life and ICICI rate better, though HDFC has a premium tier.

Easiest approval if you have had salary credits for the last 6 months to 2 years.

**Saral Jeevan Bima** — the no-proof route:
- Age 18–65, **no education or income proof required**, up to ₹25 lakh
- Offered by SBI Life, PNB MetLife, ICICI Pru and others
- The note flags *"check it once, I have some doubt on it"* — verify current terms before relying

## Low-cost cover most people miss

| Scheme | Cost | Cover | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank debit card cover | Included | up to ₹10 lakh | Must **use the card once a month** to stay eligible |
| PMJJBY | ₹500/year | ₹2 lakh | Age under 50 |
| PMSBY | ₹20/year | ₹2 lakh | Accident only, age limit 70 |
| Post Office Group Accident (GAG) | — | Accident only | Age under 65, needs a PO savings account |

Check your own bank's page — e.g. SBI publishes its debit card insurance cover.

## Health insurance shortlist

- **HDFC Ergo Optima Secure** — under 30
- **Care Supreme Direct** — older parents; riders let you **buy down the waiting period**
- **Niva (Reassured Bronze 2.0+)** — best premium *if you disclose honestly*

## Claim rejection — the operational rules

These are the notes' "points to remember to eliminate rejections":

1. **Intimate the insurer 48 hours before planned surgery**
2. **Within 24 hours** for emergencies
3. Keep **every prescription**
4. Submit **all cash-paid bills within 30 days**
5. **Renew before 30 days** of expiry
6. If mishandled, escalate via the grievance route (the note names Policybazaar)

The note also flags that health insurance rules changed and that single/newly-married and parent
cover need separate treatment — both left open.
