# US ETF selection

From *Comparison of ETFs* [1], *Where to invest* [2], *ETFs category* [3], *6 best ETFs* [5],
*Vanguard ETFs* [18].

## The core call

**VOO and VTI are treated as interchangeable** — *"invest in VOO or VTI, any one of those, both
are same."* The note-taker holds **VTI**.

`VOO` is described as the best S&P 500 vehicle, ahead of QQQ, SPDR, Schwab and Fidelity's 500
funds for that role.

## The six-ETF set [5]

| Ticker | Role in the notes |
|---|---|
| **VTI** | Total US market — "all over covered USA" |
| **VXUS** | International exposure |
| **BND** | Bonds — less return, less risk |
| **QQQ** | Nasdaq 100 |
| **SCHD** | Dividend |
| **AVUV** | "Big returns" (small-cap value) |

Also named: **VGT** — tech-focused only [2].

## The QQQ / SCHD tension

The notes work through this and land on a specific point:

- **QQQ** is "most considered due to higher returns" than VOO or VTI
- **But** QQQ and VOO are both already tech-heavy
- **Therefore SCHD** is the better *addition* — it diversifies away from that concentration
  rather than doubling down on it

That reasoning — *"more tech related investment done in both QQQ or VOO, better invest in this
SCHD"* — is the note's actual argument, not a generic diversification claim.

## VT

**VT** invests globally, but the note flags **returns will be lower**. Listed as good but not
chosen.

## SCHD's dividend growth

*"Schwab US Dividend ETF will grow its dividend every year"* — the reason it appears in both the
core set and the dividend work. See `usa-dividend-investing`.
